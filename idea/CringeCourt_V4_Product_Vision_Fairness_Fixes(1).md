## 1. What has changed in V4?

**Preserved without altering the team's intended distribution:**

- **75% success bonus:** A timely juror earns an additional 75% of their refundable jury-duty bond if the court reaches a verdict.
- **0.20 USDC failed-trial bonus:** A timely juror earns 0.20 USDC if a properly opened trial fails to reach a verdict because committed jurors missed the deadline.
- **Creator-funded successful jury rewards:** The party opening the case funds the possible successful-juror bonuses and separately sees protocol and AI fees.
- **Protocol income:** As originally proposed, after legitimate settlement CringeCourt keeps forfeited attendance bonds and unearned success-bonus allocations in specified branches, plus its disclosed service fees on successful trials.
- **Deadline policy:** If even one appointed juror is absent, no absent-juror penalty or final settlement happens before the agreed deadline, even if two jurors already agree.
- **Full creator refund on no verdict:** Where no valid verdict occurs, the creator receives the full USDC case charge back; independently spent user-owned compute credits and network fees cannot be presumed refundable.

**Improvements added to the product:**

1. **Random jury assignments** from an available eligible pool, rather than letting creators fill seats with friends.
2. **No self-judging**: the creator and anyone who is a party to the case may not sit on that case's jury, even via another wallet where the verification design can enforce this.
3. **Real private ballots as the V4 target**: a two-stage vote commitment and reveal, rather than a merely hidden website tally. The full duty includes revealing the vote.
4. **Immutable, disclosed deadlines** and a way to cast votes and trigger settlement that does not depend on the CringeCourt website being online.
5. **Frozen case evidence:** the same approved AI arguments and case materials for every juror; a public fingerprint is recorded before voting.
6. **System-fault protection:** no slashing for trials the platform could not fairly run. Platform-caused compensation is funded by the platform, not other trial escrows.
7. **Honest World and 0G claims:** strong human-uniqueness assurance where needed, a user-credits discount only if personal credits are actually used, and no unsupported promises.
8. **Privacy and originality upgrades** for Versus Mode, viral-case content and anime designs.

> **Principle:** Do not silently rewrite the user's economic model while addressing its fairness and reliability weaknesses. Clearly disclose its economic incentive: the protocol earns more in some missed-jury-duty scenarios. Reduce the opportunity for manipulation rather than pretending that incentive does not exist.

## 3. The product, in one minute

**CringeCourt is a comedy courtroom with verifiable human participation.**

A user files a consensual, ridiculous internet crime or invites a second person to argue the other side. Both sides' approved story is transformed into a dramatic courtroom scene. A randomly selected, World-verified jury accepts time-limited duty and puts down a refundable bond. Jurors independently cast hidden choices and then reveal them. Once the deadline is reached, the court determines whether the original jury reached a majority. Arc releases rewards or refunds according to fixed rules. The AI judge announces the **human** outcome with a ridiculous sentence and generates a shareable result.

**What makes this different from Reddit?** The content is theatrical, but the participation is governed by commitments: a fixed jury, a defined process, an objective completion deadline, a published decision rule, and enforceable money flows. It is not a legal judgment or a guarantee of factual truth.

## 4. Who uses it, and why?

### Mode A. Solo / Confession Court — the core demo

A person volunteers an embarrassing story, such as:

> “I sold my ETH to buy a mechanical keyboard because the switches sounded bullish.”

They choose **Crypto Crimes**, **Dating Crimes**, **NPC Behavior** or **Internet Crimes**. The AI prosecution and defense turn it into a funny case. A verified jury decides **GUILTY** or **NOT COOKED**.

Offer a lightweight **free AI teaser** with usage limits, and a **community jury trial** with the full paid, verified, onchain process.

### Mode B. Versus Court — the strongest social expansion

Two consenting people, such as partners, roommates or friends, disagree about the same situation.

- Side A presents their version privately.
- Side B independently presents their version privately.
- Neither can edit the other's account.
- Each reads and approves a sanitized version for publication before the court opens.
- Both appear as **Side A** and **Side B**, not necessarily their real names or wallet addresses in the entertainment UI.
- The AI produces balanced presentations. The human jury decides who made the more persuasive case.
- **50/50 case fee split** is the preferred option. If one party refuses to consent or fund their share, the case does not go public and paid contributions are refunded.
- Both participants are excluded from their own jury. The winning side gets comedic bragging rights, **not** the other side's bond or a wager payout.

The app must never claim perfect anonymity: public wallets and posted details can still reveal identities. Side A/B is a pseudonymous presentation choice, not cryptographic concealment of everything.

**Optional future enhancement:** World Agent Pass can request fresh human approval before the AI clerk publishes a Versus case. It must not publish merely because one side invited the other.

### Mode C. Viral Cases — engagement, not a cash prediction market

A browsable feed of sourced public controversies, harmless memes, historical disputes and consenting community cases. Each card offers neutral context, AI commentary and **points-only** audience predictions. No real-money betting on celebrity cases or court outcomes in the hackathon release.

Don't imply that community votes settle a real legal proceeding or turn allegations into established facts. Keep a clear difference between humor, reported facts and unresolved claims.

## 6. The revised end-to-end trial journey

1. **File case:** Creator writes a consensual case, chooses trial mode and approves the sanitized public text.
2. **See price and risks:** The app displays the jury reward pool, protocol fee, optional AI fee, creator refund policy, and juror bond rules.
3. **Pay and recruit:** Creator funds the case; verified available jurors enter a shared waiting pool. The app randomly offers seats. Only someone who **accepts and deposits their bond** becomes responsible for the case.
4. **Exclude conflicted users:** The creator and either Versus participant cannot serve as a juror for that case. Alternate-wallet participation must be tested against the verified-human identity design.
5. **Prepare the trial:** After the required seats are accepted, 0G generates both sides' approved arguments. The same frozen transcript is provided to every juror; a content fingerprint is recorded before voting.
6. **Open the court:** The app and contract show the same fixed voting and reveal deadlines. Jurors must have actual access to the evidence and a reliable ballot path before their commitments become slashable.
7. **Cast hidden ballots:** Each juror commits to one choice during the commitment window. Choices are not visible to other jurors in that window.
8. **Reveal ballots:** At the agreed time, jurors reveal their committed choices. A complete duty means **both stages were successfully completed** within their respective deadlines.
9. **Finalize:** If everyone completed duty early, the verdict can be announced early. Otherwise, finalization waits through the full agreed deadline, even if the apparent majority is already obvious.
10. **Settle:** Timely jurors get their original bonds back and the applicable reward; missing jurors can be slashed only under valid, active-case rules. No-verdict cases refund the creator.
11. **Share:** 0G can write a hilarious sentencing line consistent with the recorded verdict. The result card shows a human jury outcome and a settlement receipt, without leaking unnecessary identity details.

**Why random assignment is important:** A creator cannot simply invite three friends straight into jury seats. It reduces, but does not eliminate, coordinated collusion; randomly selected people can still conspire or vote carelessly. Jurors may decline an offer **without penalty** before accepting the seat and bond terms.

## 7. Voting privacy: our chosen V4 rule

**Chosen product target: two-phase commit–reveal voting.** This is more credible than calling a publicly visible vote “sealed.”

- **Phase 1, Commit:** Jurors select a side and submit a cryptographic commitment; nobody can infer the side from the recorded commitment alone if the scheme is correctly implemented.
- **Phase 2, Reveal:** After the commitment window closes, jurors disclose the choice matching their earlier commitment.
- **Completed jury duty** requires a valid commitment **and** a valid reveal by the relevant deadline.
- A juror who fails to reveal may be considered absent for settlement after the reveal deadline, even if they previously committed. The user must clearly see this obligation **before depositing any bond**.
- A pending ballot is not counted as a vote for the majority. We only count properly revealed valid votes.
- For an appointed jury of three, a verdict needs **two matching revealed votes**.
- If fewer valid revealed votes produce a majority, the result is **NO VERDICT**, and the original failed-case settlement model applies.

**UX safeguard:** The ballot workflow must warn jurors that they have a second reveal step. It should help them retain what is needed to reveal and visibly remind them before the deadline. Since a lost reveal secret can cause an unfair-feeling forfeiture, the team must test the reliability of this flow thoroughly.

**Fallback if hidden voting cannot be implemented safely in hackathon time:** Make votes explicitly **PUBLIC** and remove the sealed-ballot promise and animation; retain the rest of the settlement model. Do not silently deploy an insecure “secret ballot.” This fallback is a visible scope reduction, not an equivalent privacy guarantee.

## 8. Deadline and cancellation policies

**Suggested ordinary trial times (adjustable *before* the case activates):**

| Phase | Suggested default | Penalty policy |
|---|---|---|
| Recruitment | 30 minutes | No penalty for unaccepted invitations or an unfilled jury; refund all accepted bonds |
| AI preparation | Bounded preparation period | If the court cannot be prepared, full refunds, no slashing |
| Private vote commitment | 20 minutes | Jurors must commit a ballot in time |
| Vote reveal | 10 minutes after commitment phase | Jurors must reveal their own committed choices in time |
| Settlement | After all revealed, or after the last relevant deadline | Anyone can request finalization; no early absent-juror slashing |

The case's actual deadlines are visible and fixed before the active trial starts. The platform cannot shorten them once duty becomes slashable. The app may use much shorter **clearly labeled test/demo settings**, without telling users those are ordinary production windows.

If the site becomes unavailable, jurors must still have an accessible direct transaction path to perform their duty, or the team must activate a protective cancellation if fair voting becomes impossible. A public blockchain record cannot by itself prove every outage; handling platform failure needs an explicit, reviewable incident policy.

**System-caused failures:**

- Missing jury seats before recruitment expires → return creator funds and accepted bonds; nobody is slashed.
- 0G cannot prepare a fair case before the voting phase → refund creator and every accepted juror; no slash.
- Bad proof handling, invalid authorization or platform failures make an otherwise eligible vote impossible → do not blame jurors; protective cancellation and full bond refunds.
- A serious fairness incident during voting → cancel and refund. Already completed jurors receive the proposed **0.20 USDC incident stipend from the platform's own funded operating balance**, not from honest juror bonds.
- An accepted juror simply doesn't complete both ballot stages by the agreed deadlines during a legitimately functioning trial → the original forfeiture rules apply.
- A creator no longer likes the verdict → not a platform failure and not grounds to reverse a properly finalized case.

**Operational truth:** No ordinary no-show compensation reserve is required by the original formula, but platform-fault compensation **does** require separately available company funds. This cannot be guaranteed solely from hoped-for future earnings.

## 9. Original revenue model: unchanged

The figures below are the team's proposed **USDC-denominated test values**, not promised real-money returns.

For a jury of **2N + 1**:

| Rule | Amount |
|---|---|
| Stake / attendance bond per juror | **0.40 × N USDC** |
| Additional successful-trial reward to each timely juror | **75% of bond = 0.30 × N USDC** |
| Creator's jury reward pool | **(2N + 1) × (0.30 × N) USDC** |
| Reward to a timely juror if the case has no verdict due to absences | **0.20 USDC** from properly forfeited bonds |
| Protocol and standard AI fees | **Displayed separately** |

### MVP three-juror example

- Each juror deposits **0.40 USDC** (returned if duty is completed correctly).
- Each successful timely juror earns an additional **0.30 USDC**.
- Creator locks **0.90 USDC** for the three possible successful bonuses.
- Illustrative protocol fee: **0.10 USDC**.
- Illustrative standard AI service fee: **0.20 USDC**.
- **Standard creator total: 1.20 USDC**; discounted personal-0G-credit version: **1.00 USDC**, if personal-credit usage is genuinely supported.

**Terminology:** “Juror gets 0.70 USDC back” means **0.40 of their original money + 0.30 of actual new earnings**. The original stake being returned is not a profit.

### Three-person settlement table, after the final deadline

“Voted” below means **properly committed and revealed** under the V4 ballot design.

| What happened? | Verdict? | Timely juror's payout | Creator's outcome | Protocol gross amount **in addition to its stated fees** |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| 3 jurors vote | Yes, 2–1 or 3–0 | Each gets **0.40 bond back + 0.30 bonus** | Receives verdict; no case refund | **0.00 USDC** |
| 2 jurors vote alike; 1 absent | Yes, at deadline | Each voting juror gets **0.40 back + 0.30 bonus** | Receives verdict; no case refund | **0.70 USDC**: 0.40 forfeiture + 0.30 unused bonus |
| 2 jurors vote oppositely; 1 absent | No verdict | Each voting juror gets **0.40 back + 0.20 bonus** | Full creator USDC case-charge refund | **0.00 USDC** |
| 1 juror votes; 2 absent | No verdict | Valid voter gets **0.40 back + 0.20 bonus** | Full creator refund | **0.60 USDC** remaining forfeitures |
| 0 jurors vote; 3 absent | No verdict | No participation bonus | Full creator refund | **1.20 USDC** forfeitures |
| Platform-caused inability to vote | Protective cancellation | All bonds back; 0.20 compensation to completed voters from **platform-owned funds** | Full creator refund | **No forfeiture revenue** |

**Accounting detail:** When a verdict is reached with two timely votes, the creator's 0.90 reward pool pays 0.60 to those two; 0.30 unearned allocation goes to CringeCourt. The absent juror's 0.40 forfeited bond also goes to CringeCourt, as originally proposed. When there is no verdict, the creator gets the full **USDC case charge back**, including held fees; completed jurors' 0.20 bonuses come from forfeited bonds, not the creator's refunded escrow.

**Why the failed-trial minimum is covered:** With three jurors, one absent stake of 0.40 covers 0.20 × two timely jurors. Two absent stakes total 0.80, covering the single timely juror's 0.20 and leaving 0.60. A fully filled jury is a prerequisite: no absent stake can be forfeited if a juror never accepted and funded duty.

**Conflict disclosed, not changed:** The protocol earns additional money in some absentee scenarios. The team's rationale is that voluntary bonds deter abandonment. Nevertheless, the platform has a financial incentive to benefit from absence. Immutable deadlines, direct vote access, independently auditable assignment, fault protection and public settlement receipts are required to make that policy credible. **This is gross revenue, not guaranteed profit; operating costs still exist.**

## 10. World: prevent fake juries and self-judging

**The trust moment is accepting a limited paid jury seat.**

- Prefer strong **Proof of Human** assurance for a financially compensated and uniqueness-sensitive role, subject to actual participant availability and the current World IDKit capabilities. World says lower-friction Proof of Presence / selfie checks do not by themselves guarantee uniqueness, so the team shouldn't claim they do.
- Verify the proof through a secure supported process, not just by reading a frontend success message.
- Use **one consistent app-wide eligibility/role context** wherever supported so the creator and Versus participants can be excluded from their own juries even if they use other wallets; test the precise proof/nullifier semantics before claiming this protection is complete.
- Never publicly reveal a person's real-world identity. Keep any role-exclusion identifiers private and minimize stored data.
- Deny a second jury seat or second ballot by the same eligible person for the same case.
- An identity proof establishes eligibility, not impartiality. Random selection plus conflict exclusions reduces the attack surface but cannot ensure zero collusion.
- Demonstrate both a successful check and a rejected/cancelled/ineligible path in the sponsor demo.

**Fair assignment experience:** Jurors opt into a shared waiting room, receive a random case offer, read the approved category and duty terms, and choose whether to accept. A refusal before depositing is not “backing off” and cannot be slashed. Once accepted, the juror receives the fixed obligations and deadlines.

## 11. 0G: compute funding and the return-user loop

**AI is the product, not a cosmetic assistant.** The prosecutor argues, the defense responds, the judge introduces evidence and later comments on the genuine human result. Inference request IDs should be visible in the demo.

**User choices:**

| Mode | Who pays for AI work? | Illustrative total for three-juror paid case |
|---|---|---:|
| **Standard** | CringeCourt uses its own funded 0G compute account | **1.20 USDC** |
| **0G Compute Pass** | User securely authorizes spending personal 0G credits | **1.00 USDC**, plus consumed personal 0G credits |

**No fake discount:** Signing in does not by itself entitle someone to a discount. The user must actually fund their own inference with an approved, supported account flow and see real credit usage. Whether 0G offers third-party delegated credit spending must be confirmed; do not request unrestricted personal API keys. If unavailable, Standard works on its own and the bonus feature stays out of the MVP.

**Why return tomorrow?** Daily featured trial, new anonymous community cases, a witty personal “Rap Sheet,” juror participation history, funny streaks, and rotating courtroom dialogue/personality themes. Do not use streaks to pressure people into financial commitments; unpaid viewing and participation should remain useful.

## 12. Integrity, privacy, moderation and disputes

- Freeze the final approved transcript and evidence **before** voting begins. Each juror sees the same material; a public content fingerprint lets participants check it wasn't quietly swapped.
- AI lawyers can express opposing interpretations but may not alter an actual settled jury result.
- The onchain result records what the jurors decided; it does **not** certify that someone's allegations are true.
- Solo cases must be voluntarily submitted; Versus requires both parties to approve. Don't create public cases targeting uninvolved people.
- No doxxing, private contact details, impersonation or sensationalized real-world legal allegations.
- Use original character art, dialogue, music and courtroom layouts, avoiding close imitation of specific copyrighted franchises or TV characters.
- Public case pages may use pseudonyms. Avoid promising perfect anonymity; public wallet transactions remain potentially linkable.
- Juror payments are for **participating**, never for choosing a designated side or matching the majority.
- The paid juror model needs a real-money legal and consumer-protection review in each market before launch. Use testnet-only payment demonstrations at ETHGlobal. Renaming a deposit does not alone determine its legal classification.

## 13. How the product remains fun despite the safeguards

**No intimidating compliance UI.** Verification, bonding and ballot revealing should look like part of the anime trial:

- World verification is a dramatic **“Juror Screening”** moment, explaining its purpose in normal language.
- Accepting the seat displays **“I SWEAR TO SHOW UP (0.40 USDC)”** with a plainly visible loss condition and deadline.
- The private ballot is the **“Secret Verdict Scroll”**; a second **“Open Your Scroll”** step happens during reveal, with obvious reminders.
- When the verdict arrives, the screen does not merely announce guilt. It shows the number of jurors who completed duty, the onchain result, and each person's refund/reward receipt.
- A creator with **NO VERDICT** still receives an amusing “MISTRIAL” screen and the full paid-case USDC refund, rather than the app pretending a resolution happened.

There are only two mandatory emotional beats: **the AI arguments are funny** and **the human verdict changes something real**.

## 18. Final shareable team summary

**What we're building:** A highly entertaining AI courtroom where consensual internet disputes are decided by a small, randomly selected verified jury. Jurors voluntarily lock an attendance bond, independently complete a ballot before publicly fixed deadlines, and receive a predictable reward if the case reaches a verdict. If committed jurors disappear, their bonds can be forfeited under disclosed rules. When no verdict occurs, the case creator gets their USDC payment back. The platform keeps its original agreed fee and forfeiture-related revenue allocations, with safeguards against causing no-shows.

**What we're not building:** A real legal court, an AI that silently determines outcomes, a generic chat wrapper, a fake private ballot, an unconsented public accusation platform, or a real-money betting site for celebrity legal cases.

**Why it fits ETHGlobal:** **0G** owns the AI experience, **World** owns the trust moment, and **Arc** owns the actual conditional payment promise. The product is brainrot on the outside and auditable coordination on the inside.
